This research paper presents a study of the application of Large Language Models (LLMs) in generating formal mathematical proofs using Lean4. Despite the growing capability of LLMs to generate code, producing verifiable Lean4 code remains a significant challenge due to its strict logical and type-theoretic requirements. The methodology involved a human-in-the-loop, iterative feedback process, where users provide oversight to help guide the LLM with error messages from Lean4 Infoview. Several LLMs were tested, including OpenAI’s GPT-5, xAI’s Grok 4, Google Gemini 2.5 Flash, Anthropic’s Claude Sonnet 4, and a fine-tuned version of DeepSeek for proofs, DeepSeek Prover V2, on a series of mathematical theorems, documenting the progress and limitations. Key findings indicate that while LLMs can serve as a powerful tool in proof discovery, they often “hallucinate” deprecated or non-existent lemmas from the Mathlib, requiring significant human intervention. A comparison between the models reveals a trade-off between code-generation efficiency, cost-effectiveness, and the need for additional explanation. Ultimately, the research demonstrates that LLMS can lower the barrier to entry for formal verification by serving as a tool. Still, it is not fully capable of autonomous proof generation without human guidance.

The takeaway from this resarch paper is that although large language models are still extremely limited, newer models are becoming more capable in serving as a proof assistant. It is clear that barrier to entry for writing Lean4 code is going to drop drastically in the near future, and I have promising expectations for the near future.
